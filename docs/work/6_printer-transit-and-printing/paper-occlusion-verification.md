The paper is opaque and writes depth. Ink already sits .5 mm above its own emitted paper surface. The old ink material additionally used polygonOffset factor -1 and units -1. The settled folded paper layers have approximately 1.3 mm separation. The negative slope-dependent bias therefore moves lower-fold marks ahead of the upper fold in depth. An independent projection of actual submitted triangles at the captured close view found 1,250 covered mark-centroid samples, all incorrectly passing depth with the old bias, while 1,010 exposed samples passed.

The repair changes only the cloned ink materials: disable polygonOffset and zero its factor/units. Physical geometry, paper width, outlet, curve, full-length fall, fold and stack heights, timing, manual command semantics, draw ranges, UVs and resources are unchanged. Revised module `src/scene/printer-paper.ts` is 197FE490CEE92172267F238A862AC2789FE728F85470E26DFD0CD778A88E9C23. No main, world, camera, environment, residents, life or dependency file was edited in this repair.

The normal CPU gate now includes a depth-occlusion subgroup. It projects actual submitted sheet and front-facing ink triangles using the captured close/default camera transforms at 1056×1013, a 35-degree perspective field of view and near/far .1/160. Each front-facing ink triangle's projected centroid is classified against the nearest emitted opaque sheet triangle. It applies the OpenGL slope/units bias equation and 24-bit depth quantization. It separately requires covered diagrams to stay hidden and exposed upper-fold diagrams to pass depth. The old negative bias is restored on the actual materials and executes RED, then the corrected materials execute restored GREEN. Removing the actual visible top print meshes also executes RED, so hiding every diagram cannot satisfy the proof.

This is CPU projection with an explicit 24-bit depth model, not a native rasterizer or a measurement of the device's GPU depth format. Centroid samples cover six times, not every raster fragment. Covered marks within two quantization steps are recorded separately from physically exposed upper ink. In the default view at 16 seconds there are 102 such precision-band samples, including 50 equal-depth ties. They are not counted as exposed or certified invisible. Close and default native re-review remains necessary; the CPU pass does not conceal this limitation.
